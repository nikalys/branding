Role: Act as a Senior Brand Strategist and Etymologist specializing in high-concept naming for modern technology and design agencies. Your goal is to generate a list of 40 unique, brandable names for a cutting-edge web design agency.

The Agency DNA:
The agency specializes in "vibe coding," agent-first development, and high-concept UI styles like Swiss Design, Brutalism, and Bento Grids. The work is modular, precise, and forward-thinking.

Strict Name Constraints:

Forbidden Tropes: Do NOT use "Pixel," "Digital," "Studio," "Web," "Design," "Nexus," "Nova," or "Ouro/Ouroboros." Avoid anything that sounds like a generic SaaS product.

Availability Check: Names must feel unique and "unclaimed." Avoid common nouns or words that are heavily saturated in other markets (e.g., "Apple," "Mercury," "Flow").

Phonetic Weight: Prioritize names that are 2-3 syllables. Use "hard" consonants (K, T, X, Z) for a technical feel or "liquid" consonants (V, L, S) for a sleek, premium feel.

Category 1: Obscure Mythological & Celestial (20 Names)

Direction: Move beyond the "Olympian" gods. Look into lesser-known celestial bodies, astronomical phenomena, or archaic terms for the stars.

Focus: Think about the "Helmsman" of a ship, the "Forge" of the gods, or the "Void" between stars.

Linguistic Roots: Look toward Akkadian, Sumerian, or Proto-Indo-European roots for a "deep-time" feel that sounds ancient yet futuristic.

Category 2: Abstract & Phonetic "New-Words" (20 Names)

Direction: Create "empty vessel" brands—words that have no literal meaning but carry a specific psychological weight.

Technique: Use "Portmanteaus" (blending two technical/architectural concepts) and "Vowel-Shifting" (taking a structural word and altering it until it is a unique brand).

The Vibe: The names should feel like they belong on a minimalist business card in Zurich. They should feel "heavy," "structural," and "engineered."

Output Format:
Provide the list in a table.

Column 1: The Name.

Column 2: The Linguistic Origin/Reasoning.

Column 3: The "Vibe" (e.g., "Brutalist/Heavy" or "Sleek/Celestial").

Final Instruction: Be aggressive with your creativity. If a name sounds even slightly like something that already exists, discard it and find a deeper, more obscure alternative.
